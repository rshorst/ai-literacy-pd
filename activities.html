Short moves

Activities

Quick activities to drop into a class, a staff meeting or a workshop block. Each says whether it is about AI, where AI is the thing being studied, or with AI, where AI serves learning something else.

Nothing here needs student accounts. Every student activity runs on a teacher's screen, and Translate and check needs a speaker, not a tool.

For students

1

Unaided first

With AI25 min. Any subject.

Students do the task cold: a summary, an explanation, a set of predictions. Then they run the same task through the model and mark up the difference in two colours: what it added, what it flattened. The comparison is the assignment, not either version.

Ask
What did it make smoother, and what did that smoothing cost?
Dimensions
Knowledge-construction, epistemic.

2

Read the hidden prompt

About AI20 min. Teacher screen.

Ask an image tool for a group of people, then show students the expanded prompt the system actually sent, which usually contains descriptions nobody typed. The system edited the request before answering it.

Ask
Who decided what to add, and what would you have asked for instead?
Dimensions
Algorithmic, data. Computation and Constraints.

3

Critique the critique

With AI30 min. English, Social Studies.

The model drafts a critique of a text students know well. Students then critique the critique, line by line, against the text itself.

Ask
Where was it right for the wrong reason?
Dimensions
Epistemic, knowledge-construction.

4

The refusal log

About AIOne week. 10 min to set up, 20 min to debrief.

Students keep a running log of every time a model refuses, hedges, softens or changes the subject. In the debrief they sort the entries: safety, liability, politeness, vagueness, no idea.

Ask
What is this system protecting, and from whom?
Dimensions
Political, epistemic. Constraints.

5

Translate and check

With AI40 min. Languages, any class with multilingual students.

Translate a short passage into a language someone in the room speaks. Check it sentence by sentence with that speaker, not with the model.

Ask
What went missing that was not a word?
Dimensions
Relational, epistemic, data.

6

Source trail

With AI30 min. Any research task.

Ask for five sources on a topic. Students verify each: does it exist, does it say what the model claims, is the author real. Tally the results on the board across the class.

Ask
What would it take for you to trust a reference you did not check?
Dimensions
Knowledge-construction, epistemic. Calibration.

7

Three drafts, one page

With AI35 min plus homework. Any writing task.

Students keep their original draft, the model's suggestions and their final revision on one page, side by side, and write a short note on what they accepted and why.

Ask
Which suggestion did you refuse, and what were you protecting?
Dimensions
Creative, relational.

8

Whose hands

About AI40 min. Art, Social Studies, Careers.

Students read a short account of data labelling and content moderation work, then map the chain behind one output: who wrote the training text, who labelled it, who paid, who profits.

Ask
Which part of that chain is invisible in the interface, and why might that be?
Dimensions
Political, ecological.

9

Ask it about here

About AI25 min.

Students ask the model detailed questions about their own community: local history, a recent event, a place only locals would know. They mark what it invented and what it generalised.

Ask
What did it replace local knowledge with?
Dimensions
Epistemic, data.

10

Rewrite the assignment

With AI30 min. Any subject.

Give the model your actual assignment sheet and ask it to complete the task. The class reads the output against the criteria and decides what mark it would get, and what the task was really asking for.

Ask
What would we have to change so that answer would not be enough?
Dimensions
Knowledge-construction, disciplinary.

For educators

11

Run your own task through it

45 min. Staff meeting or workshop block.

Everyone brings a current assessment task. Run each through a model, read the output against your own criteria, then change one structural thing about the task, not the wording of the rules.

Ask
What part of this task was never really about the writing?
Dimensions
Assessment. Knowledge-construction.

12

One task, three levels

30 min.

Take a single task and write three versions: no AI, AI for planning only, AI throughout with the process shown. Decide which you would actually run and what you would tell students about why.

Ask
What is the reason, in a sentence a student would accept?
Dimensions
Assessment, political.

13

The document audit

30 min.

Before anyone writes an AI policy, list the documents that already exist: integrity, devices, privacy, assessment, home communication. Mark which need one new line rather than a new document.

Ask
What do we already say that covers this?
Dimensions
Political.

14

Show one, refuse one

20 min. Pairs.

Each teacher demonstrates one thing they do with AI, and one thing they will not use it for, with the reason. Collect the refusals on the board. The refusals are the more interesting list.

Ask
Which refusals do we share, and which are personal?
Dimensions
Relational, creative.

15

Ask the families first

20 min to design, then run it.

Draft five questions for parents and caregivers about where AI may and may not be used in their child's learning. Schools that have done this find support for short updates and resistance to anything high-stakes.

Ask
Which answer would change what we do?
Dimensions
Relational, political.

16

The wrong answer bank

15 min, then ongoing.

Every teacher brings one confidently wrong output in their own subject. Keep them in a shared folder and use them with students, since a generic error is not as convincing as one in your own field.

Ask
How would a student in your class have spotted this?
Dimensions
Epistemic, disciplinary. Calibration.

17

Marking the boundary

25 min.

Each teacher names one piece of work in their course that should stay unmediated, and says what it protects. Then: what would you do if you found it had been outsourced anyway?

Ask
Was the rule the point, or the relation?
Dimensions
Relational, creative.

18

What are we scoring

30 min.

Hand out a published set of questions for deciding whether a school should adopt an AI tool. Groups answer them for a tool the school already uses, then list what the questions never ask about.

Ask
Who would have to be in the room for those missing questions to get asked?
Dimensions
Political, futures.

19

Where does it run

25 min.

Find out where the data centres behind the school's approved tools are, what they use, and who lives near them. The numbers will conflict and most will be estimates.

Ask
What would you need to know to say this is worth it?
Dimensions
Ecological, political.

20

The register

20 min to start.

Begin a shared list of every AI tool staff and students actually use, including the ones nobody approved. Keep it open to families.

Ask
What is on this list that we did not know about?
Dimensions
Data, political.
