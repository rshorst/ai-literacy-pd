The day · 10:40 to 11:25

Some things worth trying in Copilot

A hands-on look at a small number of useful things generative AI can do for teaching: generating alternatives, explaining something in different ways, creating examples and counterexamples, testing instructions, looking for gaps, and helping you see a lesson from another angle. Try a few with something you actually teach and decide what is useful, what is not, and what you would want to change.

What we’ll do

  1. A short demo of a few things worth trying: generating alternatives, explaining something in different ways, examples and counterexamples, testing instructions, looking for gaps, and seeing a lesson from another angle.
  2. Try a few yourself with something you actually teach, using the links below.
  3. Decide what was useful, what was not, and what you would want to change.

The demo

Seven short demonstrations, each followed straight away by a question. The centre is a Copilot Notebook on AI literacy: what changes when you choose the sources, how it represents knowledge, and how source material becomes a learning activity.

  1. Your notebook as a curated knowledge environment

    4 min

    Copilot app → Notebooks

    Open the AI Literacy Notebook. Show its sources, add one, and ask the same question again.

    Ask: What changed when the sources changed? What can it not say, because it isn’t in the notebook?

    How to do it ↗

  2. Citation detective

    3 min

    Notebook → ask → open a citation

    Ask a question, then open the source Copilot cites.

    Ask: Does the source really support the answer, or only sit near it?

    How to do it ↗

  3. Study guide

    3 min

    Notebook → Create → Study guide

    Turn the same readings into questions, quizzes and flashcards.

    Ask: What do these questions assess? What would a student learn by answering them?

    How to do it ↗

  4. Infographic

    3 min

    Notebook → Create → Infographic

    Ask Copilot to visualize the main dimensions of AI literacy.

    Ask: What does it turn into simple categories? What relationships does it miss? Does the visual look more authoritative than its sources justify?

    How to do it ↗

  5. Teach: a lesson plan

    3 min

    Copilot app → Teach → Lesson plan

    Create a lesson for something you teach.

    Ask: What does it assume about the students, the place and what counts as learning?

    How to do it ↗

  6. Teach: modify content

    4 min

    Copilot app → Teach → Modify content

    Change the reading level or language of a text.

    Ask: Did it make the text more accessible without removing the intellectual challenge?

    How to do it ↗

  7. Study and Learn agent

    3 min

    Copilot app → Agents → Study and Learn

    Ask it the same question you asked Copilot Chat.

    Ask: How does question-led coaching differ from an answer-generating chatbot? Which one would your students choose?

    How to do it ↗

Before you try it

  • Use your school Microsoft account. Notebooks, Teach and Study and Learn are in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app.
  • Your school’s IT has to turn on Copilot Chat. For students aged 13 to 17, IT also has to enable it before they can use Study and Learn.
  • Some notebook features, such as audio overviews, need a paid Copilot licence.
  • Don’t add personal or student information to a notebook or a chat.
