Classroom situations · Case 04 · AI feedback and relationships

Did you actually read my work?

A fictional classroom adaptation grounded in published research or reporting.

An overworked teacher uses AI to draft comments on student essays. They read the work and review the generated comments before returning them. Feedback arrives quickly and is generally specific. One student asks: “Did you really read this?” Another appreciates getting advice in time to revise. The teacher wonders what students expect from feedback beyond useful suggestions.

The question to sit with

Is feedback only information that improves work? What forms of attention, recognition, and trust should it also carry?

The source

Doyle, Nash, Jakcsiova & Turner, Technology, Knowledge and Learning (2025). Interviews with 12 teachers and focus groups with 9 secondary students identified efficiencies and relational concerns; not a causal trial of learning outcomes.

Four lenses

Learning

What learning matters and what experiences make it possible?

Knowing

What counts as understanding, evidence or trustworthy knowledge?

Creating

What is being made, and how do authorship, experimentation and voice matter?

Relating

Who and what are affected? What responsibilities, permissions and relationships matter?

Across all four: What is the machine doing? What could we redesign?
